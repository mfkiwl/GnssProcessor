## 98. 2026-11-17: design note: one Galileo network per band (no code; bars pre-registered for a later build)

**Why** (item C4; sections 88, 88.1, 96 and 97): the two Galileo families are separate networks on the same satellites. They share one satellite level in the ionosphere-free direction (to 0.02 to 0.03 cycle, 88.1) and in the wide-lane direction up to a per-satellite term (up to 0.2 cycle); the C/Q family loses satellites whose level no held link ties (96) while X/X holds them, and a C/Q level resting on one link drifts (97). One network per band lets the held links of either family tie the other's satellite levels. Not built: no build until the author decides (the requesting session's order).

**The design**, along section 88's points:
1. *The satellite level, one per band.* One satellite phase-bias column per satellite and band (E1, E5a) for both tracking modes. In the ionosphere-free direction the modes share it to 0.02 to 0.03 cycle; the constant between the families (their datum difference) goes to one family's receiver phase biases, as an inter-signal constant does today (section 88 point 5). The ionosphere direction (0.10 to 0.18 cycle per band) is the slant-ionosphere level each family's code sets; the free per-LOS ionosphere keeps it in the links' states, with no state of its own.
2. *A per-satellite wide-lane term between the modes.* The wide-lane integer of a link is set through the code (the Melbourne-Wübbena direction), so each family's code level per satellite enters its wide lane: 88.1 measures their difference per satellite at up to 0.2 cycle (DOY 147: E12 −0.225, E32 +0.184), constant within a day and different between days. One state per satellite (the combined mode's code level against the pilot's in that direction), estimable where receivers of both modes see the satellite, born from the first epochs at which both modes hold it, without process noise within a day and with a slip and re-seed response (standing invariant 1: a jump in the term is an event at both edges, with counters). Without it a tie puts up to 0.2 wide-lane cycle into every chord that joins the modes.
3. *One tree per band* in the chain: `formChainFamilies` groups by band, the pilot and combined signals of a band in one group, the datum receiver elected over both modes' receivers (section 88 point 2).
4. *Ring-fix families by band:* E1/E5a as one family, so a chord may join a pilot link and a combined link, its wide lane an integer once point 2's term is applied.
5. *The stream:* one phase bias per satellite and band for both kinds of user and the per-satellite term for the users of the combined mode (or the per-signal biases with the tie inside), flags per band. The users' engine applies the term to a combined-mode user's wide lane (the requesting session's engine).

**The simulator first.** The simulator (`src/sim`) writes Galileo as C1C/L1C and C5Q/L5Q only (`src/sim/main.cpp`, and `SimConfig.cpp` accepts no other Galileo code), so it cannot carry two tracking modes of one band. The simulator step adds a tracking mode per station for E1 and E5a (C1X/L1X and C5X/L5X written for the combined stations), an offset between the modes in the ionosphere-free direction (one constant) and in the wide-lane direction (per satellite, up to 0.2 cycle, constant within a day), and the receiver-side offsets the observed data show. Its truth grades the tie before any observed day: the shared level recovered to the injected constant, the per-satellite term within its formal sigma, no wrong integer in the chords that join the modes.

**Bars, pre-registered for DOY 146 and 147** (24 h each, against `d24_n146` and `d24_n147`; the tie behind a key, default off and byte-identical off):
1. Galileo C/Q held share at least 75 % in every hour from 3 (today as low as 27 % in DOY 146's hour 20 and 46 % in DOY 147's hour 14), and its lost satellite-samples of section 96 at most a third of today's (437 and 390).
2. Galileo X/X and GPS held shares not lowered by more than 2 points in any hour from 3.
3. The two-run audit (two datum receivers, held states): no more failing checks than the same audit of today's runs in any family, and every failing check in a chord that joins the modes read in the post-fit residuals (section 83.5's test).
4. The users at the seven receivers of the second Galileo family (whole day, fixing and float): no hold wrong by the float check, near-null wide-lane errors not above today's count, Galileo reference changes fewer than 80 per run at each, and their accuracy (median of the hourly 3D median) not worse by more than 1 mm in any hour from 3.
5. Conditions: every epoch emitted; RAIM rows not above today's; the per-satellite term's hourly change at most 0.05 cycle except at a declared event.

**Reading prepared.** Bars 1 to 4 passing make the tie the C/Q family's repair and end the separate Galileo networks. Bar 1 failing with bars 2 to 4 passing says the lost satellites have a cause other than an untied level, and section 97's single-link rule comes next. The build touches the satellite column's key, the chain's grouping, the ring fix's families and the stream (section 88), and the users' engine for the combined mode's term (the requesting session's).

## 100. 2026-11-17: why a lost satellite's level stays untied (the re-attachment read, before the rule)

**Why** (the requesting session's order of the week, item 2a): before the re-attachment rule is written, which rule elects the receiver of a satellite's tree edge, why E36's edge stayed at CEBR00ESP from 10.0 to 16.5 h of DOY 147 while 8 to 12 receivers of the flagged piece saw it, and whether DJIG00DJI was inside the flagged piece where E04 and E25 kept their offsets.

**The election** (`seedIncumbents`, `network/solve/ChainDatum.cpp`; `growChainForest`, `network/ar/ArcGraph.hpp`): the chain is a two-queue breadth-first walk from the datum receiver. Last epoch's tree arcs that are still live and not slipped drain first ("a surviving old subtree re-attaches through its own old edges"); only a vertex they do not reach takes a new edge, held arcs first under `network.ring_fix.held_first`. A satellite's tree edge therefore moves only when its arc ends. At such a re-host the chain keeps the satellite's level (`network.ring_fix.rehost_keep_carry`; the trace's row `RehostUncertified`, cause `rehost_carry`), or takes the new edge's own float (cause `rehost_cold`). Nothing compares the satellite's level with the integers of the flagged piece.

**The three satellites** (`lost_edge_read.py` for the edge histories, `settled_graph.py --edges` for the piece of the edge's receiver at the lost samples):
- E36: born at NYA200NOR, re-hosted with its level carried to STJO00CAN (0.64 h), MRC100USA (1.72), JPLM00USA (2.88), PALM00ATA (3.66), MAC100AUS (5.89), cold back to PALM00ATA (6.18), carried to CEBR00ESP (9.16), DJIG00DJI (16.99) and SEYG00SYC (19.05). The CEBR00ESP arc lived from 9.16 to 16.99 h, so the walk kept it; CEBR00ESP was inside the flagged piece at 11 of E36's 15 lost samples there.
- E04: at DJIG00DJI from 7.88 to 14.14 h, its level carried from PALM00ATA; DJIG00DJI inside the flagged piece at 12 of 13 lost samples there.
- E25: at MRC100USA from 4.14 to 11.29 h (carried from PALM00ATA) and at DJIG00DJI from 11.29 h (carried from MRC100USA); the receiver inside the flagged piece at 13 of 14 and 7 of 7 lost samples.
- PALM00ATA, which hosted all three earlier, is outside the flagged piece at every lost sample it hosts (10, 7 and 11).

**Reading.** The tree edge's receiver is not the mechanism: it sits inside the flagged piece at most lost samples. The satellite's level is the mechanism: carried through uncertified re-hosts from a host the piece does not reach (PALM00ATA here), it keeps an offset from the piece's integers, every candidate link is refused against that offset (section 96), no held tie forms, and the walk keeps the edge while its arc lives. The repair therefore re-expresses the level through the piece: a re-host onto a candidate link with that link's own float in the piece's frame, as a cold re-host takes it, not a move of the edge with the level carried.

## 102. 2026-11-17: the re-attachment rule: a lost satellite's level re-expressed through the flagged piece (pre-registered)

**Why** (the requesting session's order of the week, item 2, on the author's decision "Yes, add it"; sections 96.1, 100.1 and 99.1): a lost satellite holds no tie to the flagged piece because its level was re-expressed, at its last re-host, through one link whose float was off (a young link, or one receiver's code-set wide lane), and the walk keeps that edge while its arc lives; its converged links agree with each other and are refused against it. The rule moves the satellite's tree edge onto the link at the centre of those converged links, so that its level is re-expressed through the piece. It is general: every held family (GPS, both Galileo families, later BeiDou).

**IRD:** the move is a re-host (IRD-4's rehost row), uncertified as today's re-hosts are; it decides no integer, so IRD-5's certification is not touched.

**The rule** (key `network.ring_fix.reattach`, default off and byte-identical off; needs `network.ring_fix.settled_flags`):
1. *Who.* After each epoch's settled flags, per held family: a satellite of the family's tree that is not settled and holds no tie (no held chord, no certified tree edge) for at least 600 s.
2. *Its candidates.* Its links this epoch whose receiver is in the flagged piece and not below the satellite in the tree (a receiver of its own subtree cannot carry it), with a float that has passed the ring fix's window and whose ionosphere-free sigma is at most 0.10 cycle (the gate's own bound), not slipped or latched this epoch.
3. *Agreement.* At least 3 candidates, whose fractions in the chain frame agree in both directions: circular standard deviation at most 0.08 cycle (ionosphere-free) and 0.10 cycle (wide lane) over the candidates (section 96: 0.04 to 0.07 and 0.07 to 0.08 on the observed days).
4. *The move.* The candidate nearest the candidates' circular medians in both directions (the smallest |d_if| / 0.08 + |d_wl| / 0.10) becomes the satellite's tree edge at the next election, in every band of the family: the satellite's incumbent edge is not seeded and the chosen arc is taken first (a lead arc of rank 1, `growChainForest`). The pin takes the re-host draft as today: the new host link's rounded float, an unknown step, the moved side re-referenced. No integer is decided: the satellite is unsettled, its counters step at every emission, and its links are then decided by the unchanged gates and persistence. After a move the 600 s count restarts.
5. *Events and counters* (standing invariant 7): a `RINGFIX reattach` event at the request (satellite, chosen receiver; the family, the old host per band, the count of candidates, the two medians and spreads) and `reattach_applied` or `reattach_missed` per band at the election; run-end counters of requests, moves applied and missed, and of moved satellites flagged within 30 min.
