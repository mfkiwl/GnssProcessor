## 12. 2026-11-14: ring-by-ring integer fixing down the chain, true integers first (pre-registered)

**The author's words.** The idea, verbatim: "Why don't we fix the "one link" then fix "two links" from root and continue fixing down the chain?" The question as put by the requesting session: "Test ring-by-ring integer fixing on the simulator, with true integers first?" The answer, verbatim: "Yes, test it". What was described to the author before the answer: two cheap cases, neither of which builds an integer search: (1) give the second ring's links their true integers and check that the third ring becomes as good as the first (0.02 cycle), then the next ring; (2) let plain rounding pick the integers in that order and count the wrong ones against truth. It was said to be card IRD's step 5 done in a specific order, and to touch the do-not-build ruling. The author is away for several hours: "From the results of the test, assign work to Opus, while I am away for the next several hours." Choices the scope leaves open are made in the author's absence and listed as such below.

**The scope, as the requesting session reads it (a reading, not the author's words).** A simulator measurement on the adopted arm (the wet delay carried). Truth-fed pins and would-be rounding counted against truth. No integer search, nothing armed, no reference configuration or ledger verdict changed. The do-not-build on integer resolution in the network stands for anything beyond this measurement until the author rules on its results. Any key is sim-only, default off, with an UNVALIDATED ledger row, and byte-identical when off.

**The rings.** On the chain's tree the root receiver has depth 0, and each link joins a receiver and a satellite. A link's ring is the larger depth of its two vertices: ring k holds every link, tree link or chord, from a depth-k vertex up the tree, and ring 1 is the root's links, all of them tree links. On the bed without re-seats (`irdu_nr`, the third cut of section 11.1; one tree for the whole run, root S30000SIM, depth 5) the chords number 54, 130, 94 and 94 band rows in rings 2, 3, 4 and 5, beside 174 tree-link band rows (`config/sim/irdu/fpin_nr3_all.csv`). The requesting session's link counts, re-graded here to the digit: on that bed the receivers at depth 2 see a median of 2 satellites of ring 1 (1 to 4; two receivers see four or more), the satellites at depth 3 a median of 4 receivers nearer the root, the receivers at depth 4 a median of 3 satellites, the satellites at depth 5 a median of 9 receivers; on r3c at 110 min (473 arcs, 89 tree edges) the medians are 2 (1 to 7, 14 with four or more), 8, 6, 12, 7 and 16 down to depth 7. Strict depth order therefore starts weak: half the depth-2 receivers tie to the root's satellites through one or two links.

**The instrument: the oracle pin in the chain's frame** (`network.cycle_slip.oracle_pin_frame_file`; new, simulation only, default empty, ledger row UNVALIDATED). The simulation-only oracle pin (a `network.inject` event of kind `oracle_pin`, `docs/record/SPEC_oracle_pin.md`) holds float ambiguities at truth through one stiff diagonal prior row per pinned column (sigma 1e-4 m). Its existing target fits station offsets in the reference configuration's anchor frame. The chain needs no fit: every tree link is pinned at an integer target, so a chord's absolute ambiguity in the chain's frame is λ times the integer N~ = N_true(chord) + Σ c (N_true(path link) − k_link), which the simulator's truth and the tree's targets give offline (`scripts/ird_frame_census.py`, `frame_integer`). A chord's carried value over its wavelength equals the chord table's float to 5e-7 cycle (checked on `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3`), so a pin at λ N~ sits in the frame the chain estimates in. The new key names a file (`scripts/irdu_frame_targets.py`: station, sat, signal, frame_int, role, ring) and replaces the fit by it. Every listed link the chain pins this epoch is checked at λ N~: a chain pin at any other value means the frame has stepped, and every frame pin is released for good. Every other listed link in scope is held at λ N~. A link whose true integer changes (a true slip) is released for good. Counters and identities: the oracle pin's per-arc `pinned` and `released` event rows (both edges, standing invariant 7) and run-end counts of links checked at their integer, tree links of the file that the chain no longer pins (then held by the oracle), links released at a true slip, and the epoch of a frame step. Without the key the oracle pin is unchanged; without an `oracle_pin` event the pin object is never built, so a run without the event is byte-identical (gate: the adopted arm on the bed without re-seats and on r3c reproduces `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3` and `_rap_irdu_zw_r3c` whole on the new build, `gnss_processor_irdu_fp2.exe` (sha256[:16] `269fd459a98fb467`, this branch plus the key): IDENTICAL on both, `nom_td_gate.py --whole --mask-time`, runs `_rap_irdu_fp2off_zw_nr3` and `_rap_irdu_fp2off_zw_r3c`). Unit tests: `tests/network/test_oracle_pin_frame.cpp` (the parser, links pinned and checked, a re-host onto a link at its integer keeping the frame, a step latching the release, the release at a true slip); on the Debug build its 8 cases and the 22 existing oracle pin cases pass, 30 of 30, and the neighbouring suites (contributions, station build, inject) 127 of 127. The 0.1 h smoke of Q1 (`_rap_irdu_smk_q1_nr3`) arms the mode: 372 chord rows held, 2 604 prior rows injected, every tree link found at its integer, the frame unchanged, nothing released.

**Q1, the upper bound.** Case: A1krhRsmh (the adopted arm) on `irdu_nr` with the setting group `fpin_nr3_all` (every chord held at its true frame integer from the run's first epoch; the pin acts from the end of the leveling warm-up), read against `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3` (the adopted arm's reference row on this bed). Gate Q1-G0: the run-end counts show prior rows injected, the frame stays the file's for the whole run, no link is released at a true slip, and gate G-B0 holds; on this quiet static bed no slip and no re-seat occurs, and the counters show it at zero. Readings: STREAM, a stream reading: D_WL and D_NL per-pair offsets by depth class at 30 to 45, 60 to 75 and 105 to 120 min, and the steps; card IRD's W bars at their original values over hour 2 in every depth class (the wire grade's `--split depth`), the per-band values beside them without a bar. USERS, a user reading: section 9's readings at the five users, against a truth wire on the bed's own satellites (`_rap_irdu_tw_nr3`: `scripts/simnet_truth_wire.py` with the bed's products as the template, so its users read the same 29 corrected satellites). Expectation: with every link at its integer, each satellite's and receiver's biases are fixed by all of their links, so the stream reads the truth stream's level at every depth (D_WL and D_NL offsets p50 at most 0.03 cycle at every depth at 105 to 120 min; W-WL and W-NL pass in every depth class), and the users read the truth wire's level. Pass rule on the stream: W-WL and W-NL pass in every depth class over hour 2, and D_NL p50 is at most 0.05 cycle at every depth at 105 to 120 min. Pass rule at the users: settled pooled WL and N_1 at most the bed's truth wire's plus 0.05 cycle, HELD at least half the bed's truth wire's, WRONG and UNRESOLVED 0. When the bed's truth wire itself fails section 9's absolute bars, the bed limits what users can show, and Q1's user verdict is read against that truth wire only. Stop rule: when the stream passes and the users fail against the bed's truth wire, the missing piece is not integer resolution; the card stops, traces why and reports before Q2.

**Q2, propagation.** Cases: `fpin_nr3_r2` (the chords of ring 2), `fpin_nr3_r23` (rings 2 and 3), `fpin_nr3_r234` (rings 2 to 4), and Q1's all. Reading: D_WL and D_NL per-pair offsets by depth class and 15 min bin. Question: with ring 2 at its integers, does the depth-3 class reach the depth-1 class (within 0.01 cycle on both channels), and from which 15 min bin; with rings 2 and 3, the depth-5 class. Expectation: the depth just below the last pinned ring gains most, since its parent receivers are fixed by several integer links; a vertex tied to the fixed rings by a single link gains nothing, since its receiver phase bias still rests on that one link.

**Q3, would-be rounding (measure only).** At each stage (no pins: `_rap_irdu_zw_nr3`; ring 2; rings 2 and 3; rings 2 to 4), the chord table's floats of the next ring's chords against their frame integers (`fpin_nr3_all.csv`) at 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 min: rounding right on the wide lane, on N_1 through the ionosphere-free row with the wide lane rounded, and on both bands band by band; the formal sigma and |e| / sigma. Strict depth order (the next ring's chords) against best connected first (the same number of chords not yet pinned, taken from the receivers with the most links already at their integers). This is the cascade's step-by-step success rate without building the cascade. Expectation: the wide lane rounds right far more often than N_1 (section 11.2's A), and the rate rises with time and with the number of fixed links at the chord's receiver.

**Q4, re-seats onto fixed links** (only after Q1 passes on the stream). C1 and C2 of section 11.1 with every link at its integer (`irdu_nr_c1` and `irdu_nr_c2` with `fpin_nr3_all`), read against Q1. With the new host link held at its integer, the re-host's rounded target is that link's frame integer, so the frame is kept and the oracle keeps every pin (the old host link, while carried, is held at its own integer). The chain still releases the moved side's carries (the certified branch stays held), so the pins stand in for kept carries. Readings: the carries erased (CHAIN `cut_release`), the moved pairs against truth by window (as in section 11.4's annotation), the stream's bars and the users. r3c with its 36 re-seats per hour needs the frame integers of links that rise during the run, which a static file cannot give: not built here, and named in the report.

**Read against the IRD family.** This is IRD-5's subject (re-seats certified from the network's own integers), done in a specific order; IRD-2's frozen rule left IRD-5 unbuilt and certification pair-only. The report says what each reading repeats, extends or contradicts in cards IRDB to IRDG and in card FCF (the fix-and-hold that failed on a datum that was not integer-recovering), and reads card IRD's stream bars and K-a to K-e beside section 9's.

**Decisions made in the author's absence, for review.** (1) The instrument extends the existing simulation-only oracle pin instead of adding a second truth-fed mechanism. (2) A link's ring is the larger depth of its two vertices. (3) Q1 runs before the rings, in the order the requesting session set. (4) At the users the thin bed is read against a truth wire on the bed's own satellites. (5) The frame file's integers stay valid while every chain pin on a listed link sits at its integer; a re-host onto a link at its integer keeps them. (6) Q4 on r3c is not built.

**Limits.** No integer search; nothing armed; no reference configuration or ledger verdict changed; the hold of 2026-11-02 on the certified re-host branch stands; the do-not-build on integer resolution in the network stands beyond this measurement.
