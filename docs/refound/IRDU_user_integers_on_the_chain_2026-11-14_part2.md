## 11. 2026-11-14: follow-up pre-registered: why the receiver phase bias stays loose although carried, and whether carried information keeps the frame through a re-seat

**The question.** The author, 2026-11-14, after section 10: "I also don't understand why the receiver phase bias is estimated fresh each epoch. I thought we carried information between epochs and basically constrain the nearly time-constant parameters, like receiver phase bias. We can also test this in sim." and "the sim is so much faster, it seems like we should target our effort in the sim first before the "observed" (you call "field" which I don't like) data." Simulated scenarios come first, observed data later.

**Correction to section 10, item 3, re-graded on its own runs before anything new ran.** "Re-estimated every epoch" is wrong as written for the receiver phase bias: it is carried. The run-start parameter table of `_rap_irdu_ch_r3c` (`main_log.log`, lines "Network (param model)") lists the receiver phase bias as carried in the station block (random walk 3e-6 m^2/s; response erase_and_rebirth at a slip or a handover), the ambiguities as carried (q 0) and the satellite phase biases as carried (random walk 1e-12 m^2/s). The estimated parameters that are not carried are the slant ionosphere (`network.carry_iono_in_block` off), the zenith wet delay (`network.carry_zwd` off) and the receiver clock: those are the ones set from each epoch's data. The trace of the requesting session reproduces exactly on the three runs of section 10 (`network_rpb_states.csv`). The bias's formal sigma per band (p50 over unpinned rows) falls from 26.9 m at the first epoch to 11.3, 5.6, 4.35, 3.2, 2.45 and 2.02 m at 5, 15, 30, 60, 90 and 119 min (identical with q 1e-9; 0.02 m from 15 min on the time link, the double count of card IRDG). At the first epoch it is 22.2 m on L1C and 36.4 m on L2W, a ratio of 1.64, close to (f1/f2)^2 = 1.65: the receiver's code-bias and ionosphere direction, which the ionosphere-free combination does not contain. The ionosphere-free step of a station's two bands between epochs, by the time since the row's last Birth or RehostUncertified, is p50 0.258 m and p95 2.06 m in the first 5 min, 0.035 and 0.147 m from 5 to 15 min, and 0.019 to 0.021 m (p95 0.066 to 0.078 m) beyond 15 min, about the random walk's own size (9.5 mm per band and epoch); with q 1e-9 the late floor falls to 0.0046 and 0.040 m while the first 15 min do not change. The 120 station-signals carry 118 Birth and 96 RehostUncertified rows in 2 h. The bias is carried, starts at about 27 m per band, restarts at every release, and carries its large steps in the first 15 min after a restart.

**A hypothesis to test (from the requesting session, not a finding).** At a receiver between two tree edges (to its parent satellite and to a child satellite) the receiver's phase bias enters both rows of one epoch and cancels between them. What a hop transfers to the child satellite is then the receiver's wet-delay error times the difference of the two mapping functions (the ionosphere-free part) and the difference of its two slant-ionosphere levels; with the ionosphere free per line of sight, each level is set through code on that single line of sight. If so, constraining the receiver phase bias cannot remove the wander. The same algebra, written out for this card: a chord's float ambiguity absorbs any constant on its line of sight, so in the geometry-free direction a satellite's phase bias is tied to the root through the code of the tree edges on its path only (the receiver's code bias, like its phase bias, cancels at a hop). The averaging error of each edge falls as 1/sqrt(the edge's age) and the errors add along the path. On that reading a satellite deep in the tree converges more slowly than one near the root even without a re-seat, and every re-seat restarts the averaging of the edges it replaces.

**The answer to the author's question.** The receiver phase bias is carried, not re-estimated each epoch: its formal sigma stays at metres only along the receiver's code-bias and ionosphere direction, which cancels between satellites, and constraining it harder (random walk 1e-9) changes nothing in the stream or at a user. What the carried information cannot survive as built is a re-seat: every release erases the moved subtree's carried floats, which then take about 45 min to re-converge in the narrow lane even when the integer was right, and r3c has a re-seat every 100 s, so its stream never settles. Without re-seats the stream converges and stops moving, but within 2 h its narrow lane becomes integer only near the root (0.02 cycle on the root's pairs, 0.09 at depth 3, 0.20 at depth 5), and the simulated users still hold no correct integers.

**The routes of section 10.** The measurements favour route (c) first: fewer releases, or releases that keep the carries where the integer is right (A: right for 97 % of wide lanes and 72 % of narrow lanes; C: the release, not the integer, costs the 45 min). They support route (b) next: without re-seats the time to converge grows with depth. Route (a), the chords on integers, would remove the depth dependence; the network's own chord floats are honest but not precise enough within 2 h to certify at the present bar. All three are the author's decisions.
