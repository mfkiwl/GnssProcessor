- Process noise: a random walk of q = 1e-09 m²/s (`network.rx_cb_carry_q`), carried in the station block and re-seeded after a gap of 900 s; over 5.5 h it allows 4.4 mm (1 sigma).
- No step at the rise and set of satellites. Per station, the share of the delay's variation at the epochs where the station's set of third-band code rows changes equals the share of those epochs: Galileo E5b 3.0 % of the variation at 2.5 % of 36 245 station-epochs, GPS L5 6.1 % at 4.7 % of 36 109, with the median change there equal to the median elsewhere (0.033 and 0.033 cm; 0.030 and 0.028 cm); `k1b` reads the same. The model carries a per-satellite extra-band code bias (`network.sat_gf_extra_band_free`), so no per-satellite term is left for a receiver's delay to average.

- Epoch to epoch, the delay moves with the receiver's DCB at the ratio of a common shift of the station's slant ionosphere, −γ3/(γ2 − 1) (the DCB maps onto the pin bands with mu / dmu, the delay enters the third band with coefficient 1): −2.77 for GPS L5 and −2.15 for Galileo E5b; measured −2.44 and −1.78 in `k1t`, −2.84 and −2.02 in `k1b` (correlation −0.48 to −0.92). - The satellite side drifts as well: the per-signal sum of the satellites' extra-band code biases, from TOW 1800 to 21 570, moves from +1.57 to +14.66 m for Galileo C7Q (26 to 29 satellites), from −1.56 to +1.02 m for C7X and from +1.20 to +2.63 m for GPS C5Q, while GPS C5X stays at 0.00 m (21 satellites) until a satellite leaves at the last epoch.
