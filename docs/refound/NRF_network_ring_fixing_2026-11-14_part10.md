## 88. 2026-11-16: what tying the two Galileo families into one would need (a read without a build)

**Why** (the requesting session's item D(3)): the Galileo L1X/L5X family (28 receivers) and the L1C/L5Q family (27) are separate networks on the same satellites; the question is what one network would need in the code, which state would carry a satellite's offset between the pilot and the combined tracking on each band, and what its datum would be. No build; a candidate next step for the author.

**Where the families are separated** (the code as it stands):
- *The signal a station uses.* The roster (`network/assembly/SignalRoster`, `signalRosterModelledOr`) gives each station one signal per band by the priority list (Galileo: C1C, C1X, L1C, L1X, C5Q, C5X, L5Q, L5X): a station that reports the pilot uses it. Of the 73 observation files of DOY 145 with Galileo, 34 report L1C and not L1X, 39 L1X and not L1C, and none reports both on band 1 or both L5Q and L5X on band 5 (header scan).
- *The satellite phase bias.* One column per satellite and signal (`common/ParamRef.hpp`, kind `sat_phase_bias`, keyed by satellite and signal): L1C and L1X of one satellite are separate columns, each family with its own gauge. The receiver phase bias is keyed per constellation and signal as well (`StateKeys::makeReceiverPhaseBiasKey`).
- *The chain.* `formChainFamilies` (`network/solve/ChainDatum.cpp`) forms a pair family only for a constellation that tracks exactly two phase signals of different bands; Galileo tracks four, so each signal is elected on its own: four trees, four datum receivers (on DOY 144 COCO00AUS for L1C and L5Q, BOGT00COL for L1X and L5X).
- *The ring fix, the flags and the stream.* `network.ring_fix.pairs` names the two families; a chord joins two links of one family; the settled flags and the stream's records are per signal.

**The data on a tie** (`galileo_family_offset.py`, `f13_n144` and `f13_n145`, every 30 min from hour 2, the satellites flagged in both families): the fraction of b(L1X) − b(L1C) in cycles has a circular spread over the satellites of 0.10 to 0.15 cycle at every sampled epoch (20 to 25 satellites; a uniform draw of the same size spreads at least 0.22 in 95 % of draws), around a common value that moves with the two families' gauges (from +0.24 to −0.20 cycle within two hours on DOY 145); b(L5X) − b(L5Q) spreads 0.14 to 0.18. The satellites' offsets agree to about a tenth of a cycle, not to a few hundredths.

**What one network would need.**
1. *One satellite phase-bias column per satellite and band*, shared by the pilot and the combined signal, with the combined family's offset carried by its receivers' phase biases. No receiver reports both signals, so a satellite's own pilot-to-combined offset is not separable from the receivers' (a per-satellite offset state would have no observation); the tie is the assumption that the offset is one constant per band, its value taken up by the receivers of one family. A per-satellite offset state becomes estimable only with receivers that track both signals on a band.
2. *One tree per band* in the chain, spanning the receivers of both signals through the shared satellite column: `formChainFamilies` would group by band (with the pilot and combined signals of a band as one group), and the pair family rule would then see Galileo as two bands.
3. *Ring-fix families by band* (Galileo E1/E5a), so that a chord may join a pilot link and a combined link; the integer of such a chord is defined through the shared column and is an integer only if the satellites' offsets agree modulo a cycle.
4. *The stream* would carry one bias per satellite and band for both kinds of user (or the per-signal biases with the tie inside), with the flags per band.
5. *The datum* of the shared column is the present one (the phase datum and the chain's datum receiver per band); the constant between the families is absorbed by the receiver phase biases of one family, as an inter-signal constant is today, so it needs no datum of its own.

**Reading.** The tie is a structural change in four places (the satellite column's key, the chain's grouping, the ring fix's families, the stream), not a knob. Its premise is weak on today's data: the satellites' pilot-to-combined offsets agree only to 0.10 to 0.15 cycle on E1 and 0.14 to 0.18 on E5a, and part of that spread is the L1C/L5Q family's moving levels (sections 75 to 78), which a tie would spread into the L1X/L5X family. A first test without a build: the same read on hours where the L1C/L5Q levels hold (DOY 146 later in the day), and the same read with receivers that track both signals if any can be added to the roster.
