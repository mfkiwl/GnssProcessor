**The receivers' extra-band delays** (the read without a run; `k1t_n144` and `k1b_n144`, hours 0.5 to 6; scripts `ifb_riseset_station.py`, `ifb_vs_dcb.py`; `_irdu_grades/nrf_k1_ifb_riseset_station.txt`, `nrf_k1_ifb_vs_dcb.txt`).
- Process noise: a random walk of q = 1e-09 m²/s (`network.rx_cb_carry_q`), carried in the station block and re-seeded after a gap of 900 s; over 5.5 h it allows 4.4 mm (1 sigma).
- No step at the rise and set of satellites. Per station, the share of the delay's variation at the epochs where the station's set of third-band code rows changes equals the share of those epochs: Galileo E5b 3.0 % of the variation at 2.5 % of 36 245 station-epochs, GPS L5 6.1 % at 4.7 % of 36 109, with the median change there equal to the median elsewhere (0.033 and 0.033 cm; 0.030 and 0.028 cm); `k1b` reads the same. The network-wide set of satellites with an extra-band code bias changes at 22 (Galileo) and 20 (GPS) epochs, which carry 0.3 and 0.1 cm of the 7 cm summed change. The model carries a per-satellite extra-band code bias (`network.sat_gf_extra_band_free`), so no per-satellite term is left for a receiver's delay to average.
- The drifts are smooth (about 1 mm per epoch for hours) and belong to single stations: Galileo E5b DJIG −80 cm, SEYG −76 cm, GOP6 −50 cm; GPS L5 SVTL +29 cm, BOR1 −25 cm in `k1t`; the network's median drift stays within 2 cm per two hours. That is 60 to 180 times the process noise's 1 sigma: the estimate moves along a direction the data hold weakly, not the state under its noise.
- Epoch to epoch, the delay moves with the receiver's DCB at the ratio of a common shift of the station's slant ionosphere, −γ3/(γ2 − 1) (the DCB maps onto the pin bands with mu / dmu, the delay enters the third band with coefficient 1): −2.77 for GPS L5 and −2.15 for Galileo E5b; measured −2.44 and −1.78 in `k1t`, −2.84 and −2.02 in `k1b` (correlation −0.48 to −0.92). That direction is the station's geometry-free level, closed by `network.rx_code_bias_datum` at 10 m; BOGT's E5b delay in `k1b` walks −4.85 m along it (DCB +2.25 m, ratio −2.15). The net drift over hours at the other stations is not the DCB's (the DCB within 3 cm while the delay moves decimetres).
- The satellite side drifts as well: the per-signal sum of the satellites' extra-band code biases, from TOW 1800 to 21 570, moves from +1.57 to +14.66 m for Galileo C7Q (26 to 29 satellites), from −1.56 to +1.02 m for C7X and from +1.20 to +2.63 m for GPS C5Q, while GPS C5X stays at 0.00 m (21 satellites) until a satellite leaves at the last epoch. Why one signal of four keeps its sum is not read here.
