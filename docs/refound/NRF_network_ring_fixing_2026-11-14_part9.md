## 75. 2026-11-16: Galileo C/Q's moving levels on DOY 144: not the wet delay (a read)

**Why** (section 73's next read; the requesting session's order after its adoption verdict, the trace before the cascade): what the C/Q sub-network's receivers put into the C/Q satellite phase biases that the X/X receivers do not, first their wet delay against each satellite's elevation.

## 77. 2026-11-16: the third band's tree on the pair's forest (built, pre-registered)

**Why** (the requesting session's approval after section 76): the cascade's difference keeps the extra-wide-lane precision only where the third band's tree paths close over arcs the pair fixes; the third band's tree is to be grown on the pair's forest. Default off, identical when absent, the simulator first.

**The build** (exe nrf31; key `network.ring_fix.cascade_tree`, default false, needs `network.ring_fix.cascade`; ledger row UNVALIDATED). The chain's forest growth (`arcgraph::growChainForest`) takes a lead rank per arc (`ForestRules::lead_rank`): whenever an attached vertex has an arc of rank 1 or more to an unattached vertex, the arc of the lowest such rank is taken before any incumbent, held or new edge (rank 1 breadth first, a higher rank one arc at a time, rank 1 then draining again), and every other edge is taken one at a time so that a lead arc always comes first; an edge taken as a lead arc on an incumbent arc reads as kept. For a family that is the one band of a cascade's third signal, `chainCascadeLead` ranks each arc of the band's graph (`ringFixCascadeLeadRank`): 1 for a tree edge of both bands of the pair at this election (the pair's bands are elected before the third in the visit order; else the last election's trees), 2 for a link the pair holds that was a tree edge of the third band at the last election, 3 for another link the pair holds (held, its monitor not tripped, neither signal freed this epoch); 0 for any other arc, for an arc slipped on the third band and for a fresh host that is no incumbent. The band's own incumbents and new edges follow as built. A run-end clause per cascade counts the third band's tree edge-epochs taken on the pair's tree edges, on held links and otherwise. In the same build, for the observed-data read that comes after the simulator: key `network.ring_fix.cascade_log` (default false, needs the cascade; ledger row UNVALIDATED), the cascades decide and trace but never hold: a link that passes the gate for the persistence is traced as `would_hold` at every such epoch (with truth on the simulator) and its third signal stays float; a run-end clause counts them. Unit tests: suite `ChainForestLead` (a file of its own: a rank-1 arc before the incumbent, rank 2 before rank 3, a lead edge on an incumbent reads as kept, and on 200 random graphs the forest spans its component and holds every arc of a rank-1 forest) and `RingFixCascade` (the ranks).

**Arms** (exe nrf31): `t3t_sim` and `t3t_sim_r`, `t3x_sim[_r]` with the key; `t3k_sim`, `t3x_sim`'s configuration on nrf31, for keys absent identical; `t3g_sim`, `t3t_sim` in the log mode (its bar: no third-signal integer held, the would-be integers against truth, the pairs as `t3t_sim`'s); a 10-epoch smoke first.

**Bars** (the requesting session's): every held or decided third-signal integer right against truth (the producer's audit and the frame grader), none wrong; the pair families' held link-epochs within 1 % of `t3x_sim[_r]`; the fraction and odds refusals of GPS L5Q and Galileo L7Q fall by at least half; the shared signal's integers equal wherever two families hold one arc (the cascade's reference integer against the pair's); the two runs agree (the same-start audit); every epoch emitted, RAIM rows not above `t3x_sim[_r]`'s; keys absent identical. Read: what is still refused and why (persistence, arcs without the third signal). Observed data only after the simulator passes, and only as a log-mode read first.

## 80. 2026-11-16: the periodic step is the time tag's rounding (read J)

**Why** (the requesting session's read J after section 78; no run): the step of every family's satellite levels at the first epochs after the odd multiples of 337.5 s of the day; which emitted record carries it, whether the station residuals step too, and which term of the observation equation jumps.

**How.** `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/j_steps.py` on `g13_n144`: per emitted record (clock, each code bias, each phase bias, the ionosphere-free phase bias with and without the clock) the detrended step dx(t) - (dx(t-1) + dx(t+1)) / 2 less the constellation's median, at the periodic epochs against the others; the same for the station residuals (`network_station_residuals.csv`). The epoch time tags of the stream (the E records). `j_rangerate.py`: at eight wrap epochs, each satellite's clock step against its mean range rate over the roster's stations above 10 degrees (the SP3 orbit, 10-point Lagrange). Grades `_irdu_grades/nrf_j_steps.txt`, `nrf_j_rangerate.txt`.

**Results.**
- **The record:** the satellite clock. Its detrended step at the periodic epochs is twice the step elsewhere (median 1.06 against 0.50 mm for Galileo, 1.15 against 0.59 mm for GPS); the code biases (ratio 0.9 to 1.2) and the phase biases (1.0 to 1.2, the ionosphere-free combinations 1.0 to 1.1) do not step. The levels of sections 73 to 78 are the user's total satellite term (the clock less the phase bias), so the clock's step shows in every family's level.
- **The station residuals step:** the mean |s_raw| of an epoch is 0.51 at the periodic epochs against 0.21 elsewhere, and its detrended step 26 times the step elsewhere.
- **The time tag:** the stream's epoch times are off their nominal 30 s values by a sawtooth: 0 at every multiple of 675 s of the day (TOW 4050, 5400, 6750), drifting by about -1.9 microseconds per epoch to -19.6 microseconds, then +19.6 at the first epoch after each odd multiple of 337.5 s (TOW 3720, 4410, 5070, 5760, 6420), a jump of +38.39 microseconds.
- **The cause:** `TimeUtils::gregorianGpsToGpsTime` (every RINEX epoch, through `GnssTimeFromDate`) forms the Julian date in double precision, `gregorianToJulianDay` adding the fraction of the day to a value near 2.46e6, whose last-bit spacing is 2^-31 day (40.2 microseconds), and converts back to GPS seconds. The epoch time carries that rounding: 30 s is 745 654.044 such spacings, so the rounding error drifts by 0.044 spacing per epoch and wraps every 22.5 epochs (675 s, 1/128 day); multiples of 1/128 day are exact binary fractions, hence the zeros there. The same holds for `gregorianToGpsTime` (the UTC scale).
- **The term that jumps:** every term evaluated at the epoch time, dominated by the satellite's position: evaluated 38.39 microseconds late at the wrap, the modeled range moves by the range rate times that (up to about 3 cm at 800 m/s). At all eight wrap epochs the satellites' clock steps follow their mean range rate with one sign: correlation 0.43 (GPS) and 0.57 (Galileo) over 253 and 236 satellite-epochs, slope 4.6 and 6.0 microseconds, 12 to 16 % of the jump; the rest goes to the receiver clocks and the residuals. A model term evaluated at 1 s spacing is not needed: the jump is in the time argument itself, read from the stream.

**Reading.** The 675 s step is the time tag's rounding through a double-precision Julian date, up to 19.6 microseconds, which places every satellite up to 7.6 cm (at 3.9 km/s) along its track from where it was at the true epoch. It does not cancel at a user by construction: a user's epochs carry the same error (it depends on the epoch time only), but it multiplies each line of sight's own range rate, which differs between satellites and between the user and the network, and the products take up only part of the network's average (the clock steps above). A user's residuals should carry the same sawtooth, up to about 18 mm on a satellite at 900 m/s before its receiver clock and position take up part of it; its size in the users' positions is not measured here. The repair is local: form the GPS seconds from the whole days (an exact half-integer Julian date) times 86 400 plus the seconds of the day, without adding the fraction to the Julian date. It moves every epoch time by up to 20 microseconds, so every product moves: a change on the shared path, for the author to decide. It does not explain the C/Q family's larger drift (the clock is common to the two Galileo families).
