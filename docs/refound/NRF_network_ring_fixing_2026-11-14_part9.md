## 75. 2026-11-16: Galileo C/Q's moving levels on DOY 144: not the wet delay (a read)

**Why** (section 73's next read; the requesting session's order after its adoption verdict, the trace before the cascade): what the C/Q sub-network's receivers put into the C/Q satellite phase biases that the X/X receivers do not, first their wet delay against each satellite's elevation.

**How.** (1) `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/cq_trace2.py` on `g13_n144` and `g13_n145`, hours 1 to 6: per satellite, D = its C/Q level less its X/X level (each against its family's median step, narrow-lane cycles) over its longest run of constant counters in both families, regressed on m, the mean of 1 / sin(elevation) over the C/Q receivers that tie it at each epoch (elevations from `network_slip_raw.csv`). (2) `g13z_n144`, `g13_n144` with `network.trace_zwd` (a trace key: its products equal `g13_n144`'s byte for byte), and `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/cq_trace3.py`: receivers of one site, the wet delay by family, and the receivers that tie the most-moving satellites. Grades `_irdu_grades/nrf_cq_trace2.txt`, `nrf_cq_trace3.txt`.

**Results.**
- **The mapping function** (DOY 144): per satellite the slope of D on m is large, 16 to 53 mm at the zenith in either direction, with |correlation| median 0.63; but its sign changes from satellite to satellite (median slope -0.6 mm, p10 -34, p90 +40). On DOY 145 the slopes are small (p10 -9 mm, p90 +15) and |correlation| median 0.30. One zenith error common to the C/Q receivers would give one sign.
- **The wet delay as estimated** (DOY 144): GOP600CZE (C/Q) and GOPE00CZE (X/X), 4 m apart, differ by at most 0.009 m over hours 1 to 6 (first to last +0.002 m); WTZR00DEU and WTZS00DEU (both C/Q), 69 m apart, by at most 0.016 m. The 27 C/Q receivers' wet delays range p50 0.020 m and p90 0.034 m over the window, the 28 X/X receivers' 0.023 and 0.042 m; first to last p50 0.007 m in both. The receivers that tie the most-moving satellites (E04, E06, E09, E13, E21, E23, E36) move their wet delay by 0.3 to 2.4 cm.

**Reading.** The C/Q receivers' wet delays behave as their neighbours' do, so a wet-delay error of the C/Q sub-network is not what moves the C/Q satellite biases on DOY 144. The per-satellite correlation with the mapping function is not specific: a smooth drift correlates with any smooth function of time. The cause is not traced. Two candidates remain to be read: the antenna models of the C/Q receivers on Galileo (mostly Septentrio; an antenna without Galileo calibrations takes the GPS band of its frequency), and the family's lower held share on DOY 144 (71 to 74 % against 84 to 89 % for X/X, section 68.1), which leaves more of its satellites' biases float.

### 75.1 2026-11-16: the antenna models: seven receivers take Galileo E5a from GPS L2

From `g13_n144`'s setup lines (the receiver ATX source): seven roster receivers have no Galileo calibration in `igs20.atx` and take the cross-system fallback, Galileo band 1 from G01 and band 5 from G02 (GPS L2, 1227.60 MHz, standing for E5a at 1176.45 MHz, since these antennas carry no L5 calibration either): CHUR00CAN, JPLM00USA, PARK00AUS and STJO00CAN in the C/Q family, KOKV00USA, SVTL00RUS and SYOG00ATA in the X/X family. Both families have them (4 of 27 against 3 of 28), so they do not separate C/Q from X/X by count; but STJO00CAN, CHUR00CAN and JPLM00USA are among the receivers that tie the most-moving C/Q satellites (E06, E09, E23), and an L2 pattern standing for E5a puts an elevation-dependent error into the ionosphere-free phase of those links. A candidate, not a cause: the test is the C/Q drift with those four receivers left out of the C/Q family (or with an E5a calibration where one exists).

### 75.2 2026-11-16: the antenna test and the held links: neither explains the C/Q levels

**Why** (the requesting session's order I after section 75.1): the C/Q flagged segment drift with the four C/Q receivers that take Galileo E5a from GPS L2 kept out of the C/Q family, against `g13_n144`; and, without a run, whether the movement sits in the satellites tied by one or two held links.

**How.** No per-station exclusion from a ring-fix family exists, so the least change is in the data: `c14_n144` is `g13_n144` (exe nrf26) with the Galileo L1C and L5Q observation types stripped from the RINEX files of CHUR00CAN, JPLM00USA, PARK00AUS and STJO00CAN (`rapid_arm.py make --rinex "strip;sys=E;types=?1C,?5Q;stations=..."`); the four RINEX paths and the products path are the only differences in the configuration. The four receivers then have no Galileo signal of the posture's bands (they also report E6, E5b and E5) and stay in the solve on GPS alone. A 10-epoch smoke first (exit 0). The held-link read is `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/cq_trace4.py`: each flagged run of constant counters with its ionosphere-free level range (stream_seg_drift.py's levels, narrow-lane cycles), binned by the median number of the family's held links (held or decided in `network_ring_fix.csv`; tree edges not counted) that tie the satellite over the run. Grades `_irdu_grades/nrf_c14_grade.txt` (script `grade_c14.sh`) and `nrf_cq_trace4.txt`.

**Results** (hours 1 to 6; `g13_n144` in parentheses).

Held share by half hour 3.0 49.9 70.3 68.5 72.8 71.8 70.3 70.3 86.1 87.1 88.2 92.1 % (3.5 53.4 71.3 70.8 74.4 73.3 73.3 72.6 85.5 86.5 86.2 89.7); flagged share 4 52 70 71 71 69 68 71 81 80 81 78 % (4 61 73 75 73 73 74 72 81 78 78 75).
- **X/X:** p90 0.135 (0.117), largest 0.302 (0.207), share 18.5 % (11.8 %). It moved: E05's segment from 0.207 to 0.302 cycle and four more segments over 0.15 cycle (E04, E11, E19, E03). Held share within 1.0 point and flagged share within 2 points by half hour.
- **GPS:** p90 0.155 (0.157), largest 0.269 (0.272), share 18.6 % (18.6 %); held and flagged shares within 1.1 points.

**Reading.** By the order's bars the share stays above 60 % (89.5 %), so the GPS L2 pattern the four receivers borrow for E5a is ruled out as the cause. The movement sits in the satellites tied by the most held links, not by one or two, so the family's lower held share is ruled out too. The X/X levels moved when four C/Q receivers left Galileo while GPS did not: the two Galileo families are coupled through the parameters they share, the Galileo satellite clocks among them. The cause of the C/Q levels' movement is not traced. For the record, `igs20.atx` has no Galileo calibration for AOAD/M_T, LEIAR25 or ASH701945G_M in any radome, while other radomes of TWIVC6150 (SCIS), JAVRINGANT_DM (NONE, SCIS) and TRM159900.00 (SCIT) carry Galileo E1 and E5a.

## 76. 2026-11-16: the cascade's fraction refusals: the third band's tree leaves the pair's holds (a read)

**Why** (section 71.1's open read, the cascade's next step after order I): where GPS L5Q's 19 557 and 18 388 fraction refusals on the simulator come from.

**How.** `_irdu_grades/scripts_s20/cascade_frac.py` on `t3x_sim` and `t3x_sim_r` (exe nrf29; GPS L5Q from L2W, Galileo L7Q from L5Q): every cascade row of `network_ring_fix.csv` by state; for the fraction refusals the sigma of the difference f_ref - f_3, its fraction and the link's age (seconds since its current run of cascade rows began); the would-be n_3 = n_ref - round(f_ref - f_3) of every held, decided and refused row against truth in the frame of its epoch (`scripts/ird_frame_census.py`); and whether the link's tree path (satellite to station, `network_chain_edges.csv`) is the same set of arcs in the reference's band and in the third's and, where not, whether every arc of the third band's path is a tree edge of the reference's band or a link a pair holds at that epoch. Grade `_irdu_grades/nrf_cascade_frac.txt`.

**Results.**
- **Truth:** the refused would-be integers are wrong in a share: GPS L5Q fraction 11.7 and 9.1 % (2 286 of 19 557, 1 681 of 18 388), odds 6.1 and 9.9 %; Galileo L7Q fraction 21.3 and 34.9 %, odds 10.2 and 9.3 %. Every held and decided row right.
- **Sigma:** of GPS L5Q's fraction refusals 95.7 and 90.2 % have a difference sigma over 0.10 cycle (Galileo all of them), |fraction| p50 0.21; the refused links are 2 430 and 3 420 s (p50) into their run of cascade rows, so the refusals are not the start of an arc alone.
- **Paths:** every fraction and odds refusal, GPS and Galileo on both runs, is on a link whose third band's tree path differs from the reference band's and passes through an arc no pair holds (neither a tree edge of the reference's band nor a held link); there the difference sigma p50 is 0.23 to 0.37 cycle. Where the two paths are the same, or every arc of the third band's path is a reference-band tree edge or a held link, the difference sigma p50 is 0.011 to 0.026 cycle and nothing is refused for the fraction or the odds (those links wait only on persistence). For the pair the two bands' paths are the same on every row (`t3x_sim`, GPS L1C against L2W).
- **The trees:** the three GPS bands' trees span the same 32 satellites and 60 stations. A constellation with three phase signals forms no pair family (the run line: zero pair families, every constellation-epoch elected per signal), so each band grows its own tree. The L1C and L2W trees are identical at all 360 epochs of the trace, the L5Q tree at 68; L5Q has 111 uncertified re-hosts against 108 on each pair band.

**Reading.** The difference f_ref - f_3 has the extra-wide-lane precision only where the pair's holds fix the slant ionosphere along the third band's ring. A tree edge of the third band on an arc no pair holds puts that arc's float ionosphere into every chord through it (about 0.29 cycle per metre of L1 slant delay for GPS L2 less L5, 0.18 for Galileo E5a less E5b), and the gate refuses those chords rightly. Another ring of the same link gives no more precision: its third-band float is the link's float plus known integers, and the vehicle's covariance already conditions on every hold. The remedy is in the third band's tree: grown on the pair's forest (its edges taken from the pair's tree edges and held links on the arcs that track the third signal), every chord of a held pair link would close over arcs the pair fixes. That build (default off) is proposed as the cascade's next step, ahead of the re-expression of a held third signal across a cut of known step: the refusals it addresses are 25 809 and 23 737 GPS L5Q link-epochs, against 11 502 and 9 528 waiting on persistence.

## 77. 2026-11-16: the third band's tree on the pair's forest (built, pre-registered)

**Why** (the requesting session's approval after section 76): the cascade's difference keeps the extra-wide-lane precision only where the third band's tree paths close over arcs the pair fixes; the third band's tree is to be grown on the pair's forest. Default off, identical when absent, the simulator first.

**The build** (exe nrf31; key `network.ring_fix.cascade_tree`, default false, needs `network.ring_fix.cascade`; ledger row UNVALIDATED). The chain's forest growth (`arcgraph::growChainForest`) takes a lead rank per arc (`ForestRules::lead_rank`): whenever an attached vertex has an arc of rank 1 or more to an unattached vertex, the arc of the lowest such rank is taken before any incumbent, held or new edge (rank 1 breadth first, a higher rank one arc at a time, rank 1 then draining again), and every other edge is taken one at a time so that a lead arc always comes first; an edge taken as a lead arc on an incumbent arc reads as kept. For a family that is the one band of a cascade's third signal, `chainCascadeLead` ranks each arc of the band's graph (`ringFixCascadeLeadRank`): 1 for a tree edge of both bands of the pair at this election (the pair's bands are elected before the third in the visit order; else the last election's trees), 2 for a link the pair holds that was a tree edge of the third band at the last election, 3 for another link the pair holds (held, its monitor not tripped, neither signal freed this epoch); 0 for any other arc, for an arc slipped on the third band and for a fresh host that is no incumbent. The band's own incumbents and new edges follow as built. A run-end clause per cascade counts the third band's tree edge-epochs taken on the pair's tree edges, on held links and otherwise. In the same build, for the observed-data read that comes after the simulator: key `network.ring_fix.cascade_log` (default false, needs the cascade; ledger row UNVALIDATED), the cascades decide and trace but never hold: a link that passes the gate for the persistence is traced as `would_hold` at every such epoch (with truth on the simulator) and its third signal stays float; a run-end clause counts them. Unit tests: suite `ChainForestLead` (a file of its own: a rank-1 arc before the incumbent, rank 2 before rank 3, a lead edge on an incumbent reads as kept, and on 200 random graphs the forest spans its component and holds every arc of a rank-1 forest) and `RingFixCascade` (the ranks).

**Arms** (exe nrf31): `t3t_sim` and `t3t_sim_r`, `t3x_sim[_r]` with the key; `t3k_sim`, `t3x_sim`'s configuration on nrf31, for keys absent identical; `t3g_sim`, `t3t_sim` in the log mode (its bar: no third-signal integer held, the would-be integers against truth, the pairs as `t3t_sim`'s); a 10-epoch smoke first.

**Bars** (the requesting session's): every held or decided third-signal integer right against truth (the producer's audit and the frame grader), none wrong; the pair families' held link-epochs within 1 % of `t3x_sim[_r]`; the fraction and odds refusals of GPS L5Q and Galileo L7Q fall by at least half; the shared signal's integers equal wherever two families hold one arc (the cascade's reference integer against the pair's); the two runs agree (the same-start audit); every epoch emitted, RAIM rows not above `t3x_sim[_r]`'s; keys absent identical. Read: what is still refused and why (persistence, arcs without the third signal). Observed data only after the simulator passes, and only as a log-mode read first.
